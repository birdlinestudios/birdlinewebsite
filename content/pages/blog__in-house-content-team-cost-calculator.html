Why Wouldn’t I Just Hire a Full-Time Team?

It is one of the first questions brands ask us, and the honest answer is a number. So we built a calculator: the team your volume needs on payroll, next to the same output on a Birdline partnership.

It is one of the first questions a brand asks us: for that money, why wouldn’t I just hire someone full-time?

Sometimes that is the right call. If the work is one discipline, forty hours a week, for years, hire the person.

The trouble is that content stopped being one discipline. A month of paid social takes video edits, statics, motion, scripts, someone running the queue and someone looking at what performed. One hire covers one of those. So the fair comparison is not Birdline against an editor. It is Birdline against a team.

Skip to the calculator

One hire covers one discipline

Here is the team it takes once a brand has to ship every week.

  • A video editor. Cuts the ads, the cutdowns and every resize.
  • A designer who animates. Statics, motion graphics and the product render when a launch needs one.
  • A producer or project manager. Runs the queue, the reviews and the shoots.
  • A copywriter. Hooks and scripts, every week.
  • A creative director. Sets the bar and manages the people, once there are people to manage.

Nobody needs all five on day one. A ten-person brand gets by with a maker or two. By the time you are shipping forty ads a month across video and design, most of that list is on payroll.

What each seat pays

Start with who you are comparing against. Every editor and designer/animator at Birdline is senior or expert level. So the like-for-like hire is senior too, not the average person in the job.

For those two seats we use the 75th percentile from the Bureau of Labor Statistics for May 2025, its most recent figures. Three in four people in the job earn less. The support roles stay at the median.

BLS has no line for a designer who also animates, so we use the midpoint of the two jobs it does track. And these are national numbers. In Los Angeles or New York, expect to pay more.

For a second opinion, Robert Half’s 2027 salary guide puts the high end of its national range at $90,250 for a video editor and $108,500 for a motion designer, and its midpoint for a creative director at $135,000. Our editor runs above theirs, our designer sits beside it and our creative director runs low. If your market says otherwise, change the salary in the calculator.

The salary is not the cost

A hire costs more than the number on the offer letter. For full-time workers in private industry, BLS puts insurance, retirement and legally required costs like Social Security and Medicare at $10.28 an hour, on top of $41.37 in wages and paid leave. That is about 25 cents for every salary dollar, before any bonus.

Then the seat. Adobe lists Creative Cloud Pro for teams at $99.99 a month per license. Add a workstation, review tools and stock, and we budget $2,500 a person per year.

Then the drives. Video is heavy, and editors do not cut from a laptop’s own disk. They edit from fast portable SSDs, and the footage from every shoot gets backed up to a hard drive. As of this writing, B&H lists a 4TB portable SSD at about $940 and an 8TB desktop hard drive at about $280. We budget two SSDs per editor a year and one backup drive per production.

Then the search. SHRM’s 2026 benchmark puts the median time to fill a nonexecutive role at 39 days. A four-person team is four searches.

A hire is a salary, a benefits bill, a seat and a search. A team is that, repeated.

A team still needs a way to work

Hire the whole team and you still do not have a content operation. Someone has to decide where briefs live, how feedback reaches the editor, where the finals go, and how last month’s results get to whoever writes next month’s brief.

Most teams stitch that together from docs, a tracker, a review tool, a shared drive and an ads dashboard. The subscriptions are the small part. Asana’s Starter plan lists at $10.99 a person a month and Frame.io’s Pro plan at $15, so we budget $300 a person a year.

The bigger cost is the time lost in the gaps between them. In Monotype’s 2025 survey of 1,008 creative professionals, 57% of creative teams said more than a quarter of their time goes to non-creative tasks, workflow bottlenecks among them.

The Birdline App is that workflow, already built. We built it out of our own productions, every partnership runs through it, and it is included. A brief, its shot list, its deliverables and its ad results stay connected as one campaign.

It saves time and money three ways.

  • Less friction. Every brief, deliverable and final file lives in one place, tied to its campaign. Nobody digs through an inbox for the latest version, and nothing gets re-keyed into a spreadsheet.
  • Faster to execution. A Smart Brief pulls in the docs you already have and becomes a shot list and assigned deliverables. Edits start sooner, and so do productions.
  • More and newer content. Your ad results sit beside the creative that earned them, and the next brief starts from what worked. New ads launch faster because nobody starts from a blank page.

It also moves the starting line. On payroll, the clock starts with a search: 39 days to fill one seat at the median. With us, onboarding is simple and fast, and we usually start within a week. For the brand the calculator opens on, that is 111 ads shipped in the first 90 days instead of 68.

Run your own numbers

The calculator builds the team your volume calls for, prices it, and sets it beside the same output on a Birdline partnership. Every figure behind it sits under “Adjust the assumptions,” and you can change any of them.

It opens on a brand of 51 to 200 people shipping 40 ads a month, half video and half design, with four productions a year. That takes four people on payroll: $383,080 in salaries, and $493,070 once benefits, payroll taxes, software, tools and drives are in. The same output with Birdline is one dedicated senior editor, one dedicated senior designer/animator and a project manager: both packages, $14,000 a month, $168,000 a year, with the Birdline App included.

How the math works

  • Makers. One senior editor for every 20 finished video ads a month, and one senior designer/animator for every 30 statics or motion pieces. The same rule sets how many dedicated editors and designers a partnership needs, so both sides ship the same work at the same level.
  • Support. Support roles join once there are two makers. A producer or project manager for every eight makers, starting at a company past ten people or at three makers anywhere, and one more at a production a month. A copywriter from 51 employees or 60 ads a month. A creative director from 201 employees or five makers. Six productions a year call for a producer whatever the team.
  • Payroll. Salaries, plus 25% for benefits and payroll taxes, plus $2,500 a seat for software and equipment and $300 a person for workflow tools.
  • Drives. Two 4TB editing SSDs for each editor a year, $1,900, and one 8TB backup hard drive for each production, $280.
  • Birdline. Our starting prices: Video Editing at $8,000 a month and Design & Animation at $6,000 a month, one package for each dedicated creative. The project manager, script and copywriting, strategy calls, the Birdline App and the SSDs we edit from come with it.
  • Productions. A shoot costs what it costs: crew, talent, locations and gear. The calculator puts the same budget on both sides, $20,000 a production unless you change it.
  • Start. On payroll, every seat is searched at once and filled in 39 days, the median. With Birdline, work starts within a week. Each side then ships at its full monthly rate, which is generous to a new hire.

What the calculator leaves out

The estimate leaves real costs off the payroll side. It does not count:

  • Recruiting, and the weeks each seat sits open before the work starts.
  • Bonuses, raises and the cost of replacing someone who leaves.
  • The hours someone senior spends managing, reviewing and covering vacations.
  • Shared storage, cloud backup and replacing the drives that fail.
  • Office space, if the team works from one.

On our side, package prices are starting prices. The real number comes out of a call, where we look at what you need made.

When demand moves

The cost today matters less than what happens when the plan changes. Slide ads a month from 40 to 80 and watch both sides.

On payroll, every new seat is a search, then a salary that stays whether the volume does or not. When a launch passes and the queue thins, the payroll does not.

On a partnership, we add a dedicated editor or a dedicated designer/animator to your team. The project manager, the Birdline App and your library are already in place. When the calendar slows, you scale back down. Every partnership is rolling, with 60 days’ notice to cancel.

A content team that works like an employee and scales like an agency.

When hiring is the right call

None of this is an argument against in-house teams. A partnership works alongside yours.

Hire for the roles that carry the brand: the person who owns the voice, sits in the product meetings and decides what gets made. Keep them close. Put the production capacity, the part that swells and shrinks with the calendar, on a partnership that can swell and shrink with it.

And if the calculator says a hire wins for you, hire. It can. One editor at the median salary, $75,420, lands within $3,000 a year of the Video Editing package, and a junior editor comes in under it. What changes the math is the level. At senior pay, the level you get with us, that one seat runs about $40,000 a year over the package, and the gap widens with the second discipline.
