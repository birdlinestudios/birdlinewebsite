Inside a Birdline Kickoff

The first week of a partnership, and why we spend it learning how you work before we make anything.

Mike PaganFounder and CEO, Birdline
August 12, 2026·4 min read
Key takeaways
  • One call to meet the team and decide what comes first.
  • Share what you have: guidelines, past campaigns, raw footage, working files.
  • A Slack channel and the Birdline App are set up before the first brief.

A partnership that starts well is usually one where the first week was spent listening. Here is what ours looks like.

The kickoff call

About forty-five minutes. You meet your project manager and the Birds who will make your work. We learn how your team runs: who signs off, where the files live, what is coming up on the calendar and what is keeping you up at night. Then we decide, together, what to tackle first. Usually that is the next campaign; sometimes it is a backlog of footage nobody has had time to cut.

What to share

Everything you have, in whatever shape it is in. Brand guidelines. Past campaigns, the ones that worked and the ones that did not. Raw footage and project files from previous productions. Performance data if you can get it. We organize what comes in and flag what we can start using right away. More than once, the first deliverables of a partnership have been cut entirely from footage the brand already owned.

The fastest first win is usually sitting on a hard drive you already have.

Setting up the workflow

Before the first brief, two things are in place. A shared Slack channel connects both teams, with a thread per project so conversations stay attached to the work. And you are set up in the Birdline App, which is included in the partnership and open to your own projects too. Briefs, deliverables and the finished library live there, and when performance data is connected, so do the results.

We deliberately do not work inside your internal tools. Permissions, folder structures and notification settings vary from company to company, and the time spent navigating them is time not spent on the work. Slack for the conversation, Frame.io for review, the Birdline App for everything else.

The first deliverables

Most partnerships have their first edits in review within the first two weeks, and the first strategy call on the calendar for the week after. From there the rhythm sets in: briefs in, drafts to Frame.io, finals to the library, a call every other week to look at what shipped and decide what is next.

Onboarding is simple and fast on purpose. We can usually start within a week of the first conversation. The work is what takes care; the start should not.
