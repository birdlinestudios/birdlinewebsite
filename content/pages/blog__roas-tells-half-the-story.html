ROAS Tells You Half the Story. ROCS Tells the Rest.

Return on Campaign Spend adds production to the equation, so a campaign’s real return finally includes the cost of making it.

Mike PaganFounder and CEO, Birdline
August 26, 2026·5 min read
Key takeaways
  • ROCS = campaign revenue ÷ (media spend + production spend).
  • It stops cheap creative from looking efficient by default.
  • ROPS and ROCS together tell you what to make and what to buy.

Every dashboard reports ROAS. Few report what the campaign actually cost. Media is in the denominator; the shoot, the edits, the design and the talent are somewhere in finance, under a different heading, on a different month. The result is a return figure that leaves out a third of the spend.

Return on Campaign Spend

ROCS puts the whole campaign in one fraction: revenue attributed to the campaign, divided by media spend plus production spend. If a campaign drove $600,000 on $150,000 of media and $50,000 of production, the ROAS is 4 and the ROCS is 3. Both are true. Only one tells you what the campaign returned on what you actually paid.

Why the gap matters

When production is invisible in the return, it gets treated as overhead to minimize rather than an input to optimize. Teams default to cheaper creative because the spreadsheet never shows them the cost of the alternative: more media spent pushing weaker ads.

ROCS surfaces the trade. A better shoot that lifts ROAS from 3 to 4 can pay for itself several times over, and ROCS is the number that shows it. Equally, an expensive production that did not move results shows up as exactly that, instead of hiding behind a healthy media ratio.

ROAS asks how well the media worked. ROCS asks whether the campaign was worth it.

ROPS and ROCS together

We use the two numbers for two decisions. Return on Production Spend tells us what to make: which productions earned their keep and deserve more cuts, and which did not. Return on Campaign Spend tells us what to buy: whether the mix of creative and media is returning more than it costs, campaign by campaign.

Read together, they turn the strategy call into something concrete. The hero shoot has a ROPS of 18 and climbing, so we cut three more hooks from it. The spring campaign’s ROCS slipped below the summer one, so we look at whether the creative or the media mix changed. Nobody has to argue from taste.

Getting the numbers

Both require two things most teams do not have in the same place: ad-level revenue from the platform and the real, all-in cost of each production. The Birdline App connects the first and records the second as part of the brief, so the numbers appear without a quarterly reconciliation project. If you measure by hand, the method is the same; it just takes longer to get to the conversation that matters.
